A kill brick is the first thing most people script in Roblox. Here it is a few lines, and they are not the ones you know: there is no `Humanoid`, and a touch hands you the player.

Start with a level that has a floor and a player start. [Write your first script](/guides/write-your-first-script/) covers the editor's side of a script.

## A kill brick

<Steps>

1. Insert a part, name it `Lava` and size it into a low slab on the floor, on the way the player walks.

2. With it selected, press Script. In `Assets/Scripts/Lava.luau`, write:

   ```luau
   -- Sends the player back to the start when they touch this part.
   local lava = script.Parent

   lava.Touched:Connect(function(character)
   	character:Respawn()
   end)
   ```

3. Press Play and walk onto the slab.

</Steps>

You are back at the player start.

`Touched` fires once when the player starts to touch the part: stands on it, pushes against it, or walks into it when it is not solid. `TouchEnded` fires once when the player is clear of it again.

The function is handed the player's character. `Respawn` sends it back to where it started, the way falling out of the level does.

## The line that does not port

This is the kill brick of every Roblox tutorial:

```luau
--!strict
-- A kill brick the way a Roblox script writes one.
local lava = script.Parent :: Part

lava.Touched:Connect(function(hit)
	local humanoid = hit.Parent:FindFirstChild("Humanoid")
	if humanoid then
		humanoid.Health = 0
	end
end)
```

Visual Studio Code puts a red line under `hit.Parent`. Played, the touch puts a red line in Output, `Parent is not a valid member of Character "Character"`, with `Lava.luau:6` at its right end.

In Roblox the function gets the part that touched, a foot or a leg, and the script climbs from it to the character model and looks for its `Humanoid`. Here:

- Only the player touches. A part that slides into another part fires nothing.
- What the function gets is the character itself, the same object as `Players.LocalPlayer.Character`.
- The character is not in the scene tree. It has no `Parent`, no children and no `Humanoid`.
- There is no health. A script sends the player back with `Respawn` or puts them somewhere with `Teleport`.

## Tag the parts in the editor

One script on every lava part does not scale. Give the parts a tag, and let one script find them.

<Steps>

1. Stop, and delete the script under `Lava`.

2. Select the part. In the Properties panel, find Tags, type `Lava` into Add a tag and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. The tag shows as a chip.

3. Build more lava and tag it the same way. Select several parts and the tag goes on all of them.

4. Insert a part for a checkpoint: as wide as the path and tall enough to walk through. Under Behavior, switch Collides off, so the player walks through it. Tag it `Checkpoint`.

5. Duplicate the checkpoint along the course. A copy keeps its tags.

</Steps>

A tag is a word on a node. It is saved with the level, and a script finds every node that has it through `CollectionService`, as in Roblox.

## Checkpoints

Press <kbd>Esc</kbd>, which clears the selection, and then press Script. With nothing selected it makes a script for the whole level, and names the file after the level. For a level called `Lobby` the status bar says:

```text
Added a level script and made its file, Assets/Scripts/Lobby.luau.
```

In that file, write:

```luau
-- Remembers the last checkpoint the player walked through. A player who
-- touches lava is put back there.
local CollectionService = game:GetService("CollectionService")

local saved

for _, checkpoint in CollectionService:GetTagged("Checkpoint") do
	checkpoint.Touched:Connect(function()
		saved = checkpoint.Position
		print("reached", checkpoint.Name)
	end)
end

for _, lava in CollectionService:GetTagged("Lava") do
	lava.Touched:Connect(function(character)
		if saved then
			character:Teleport(saved)
		else
			character:Respawn()
		end
	end)
end
```

Press Play. Walk through a checkpoint and then onto lava: you are back in the checkpoint. Touch lava before any checkpoint and you are back at the start.

`Teleport` takes a position and puts the player's feet there, at rest. Here that is the middle of the checkpoint, and the player drops to the floor from it. It also takes a `CFrame`, and the player then faces the way the frame looks.

A place inside something solid is taken too. The player is pushed out of it over the next ticks.

## A pad that makes you faster

A tag says what a part is. An attribute says how much.

<Steps>

1. Stop. Insert a part, size it into a slab on the floor and tag it `SpeedPad`.

2. Under Attributes, type `Speed` into Add an attribute, pick Number in the list below it and press Add. Type 9 into the row that appears.

3. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> and then Script again, for a second level script. Its file has a 2 after the level's name, `Lobby2.luau`. Write:

   ```luau
   -- Makes the player faster while they stand on a part tagged SpeedPad.
   -- A pad says how fast in its attribute Speed.
   local CollectionService = game:GetService("CollectionService")
   local Players = game:GetService("Players")

   local character = Players.LocalPlayer.Character
   local walk = character.WalkSpeed

   for _, pad in CollectionService:GetTagged("SpeedPad") do
   	pad.Touched:Connect(function()
   		character.WalkSpeed = pad:GetAttribute("Speed") or walk * 2
   	end)

   	pad.TouchEnded:Connect(function()
   		character.WalkSpeed = walk
   	end)
   end
   ```

4. Press Play and walk over the pad.

</Steps>

A player walks 4.5 units a second. On the pad it is 9, and past it 4.5 again. A pad with no `Speed` doubles the speed.

`Players.LocalPlayer.Character` is the player. It is there from a script's first line, so nothing has to wait for `CharacterAdded`.

| Member | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Position`, `CFrame` | Where the player's feet are, and which way the player faces. Read only: move the player with `Teleport`. |
| `Velocity` | How fast the player moves and which way. Set it to push or throw the player. |
| `WalkSpeed` | Units a second. |
| `JumpHeight` | Units. A player jumps 1.2 units high. |
| `IsGrounded` | Whether the player stands on ground. |
| `Teleport`, `Respawn` | As above. |

A unit is a metre, so Roblox's numbers are too big here. A `WalkSpeed` of 16 is 16 metres a second, more than three times the walk.

Stop puts back the speed and the jump height the player had, and every tag and attribute a script set or took away. What you gave a node in the Properties panel is what it has again.

## Tags and attributes from a script

A script sets them too, while the level plays:

- `part:AddTag("Lava")`, `part:RemoveTag("Lava")` and `part:HasTag("Lava")`.
- `CollectionService:GetInstanceAddedSignal("Lava")` fires when a node gets the tag or a node with it comes into the level, and `GetInstanceRemovedSignal` when one loses it or leaves.
- `part:SetAttribute("Heat", 900)`, `part:GetAttribute("Heat")` and `part:GetAttributes()`. An attribute holds a string, a number, a boolean, a `Vector3`, a `CFrame` or a `Color3`.
- `part.AttributeChanged` and `part:GetAttributeChangedSignal("Heat")` fire when a script sets another value.

What a script sets is for the scripts of that play. It is never saved into the level.

## If nothing happens

- A block cannot be listened to. `Touched` on one is an error that says to convert it to a part: select it and press Convert on the Build tab.
- A part with Touch events off under Behavior notices nothing.
- A tag is case sensitive. `lava` is not `Lava`.
- Open the Output panel. A script that failed says why there, with its file and line.

## What differs from Roblox

- `Touched` hands over the character, not a part of it. Only the player touches.
- The player is `Players.LocalPlayer.Character`. It has no `Humanoid`, no `HumanoidRootPart` and no health, and it is not in `workspace`.
- `WalkSpeed` and `JumpHeight` are in metres.
- There is one player. `Players.PlayerAdded` and `CharacterAdded` do not exist.
